# Path A — Trainer Cascade

> **Instantiation layer.** A lean run-guide for organizations that have, or can assemble, a
> train-the-trainer population. Adapt activities, owners, and timelines to your context.

**When this path applies:** the routing logic in `assessment/scoring-and-routing.md` recommends
Path A when the organization has an established or partial L&D function and a credible population
of senior sellers, solution engineers, or field trainers who can cascade enablement to the wider
field.

**The core idea:** enable a small group deeply, once, then let them multiply reach. The
accelerating partner (or central L&D) enables the trainer population to the **Advisor** tier; that
population then enables the field. This is the multiplicative reach that makes enablement an
accelerator rather than a cost center.

```
Accelerator / Central L&D
        │  enables to Advisor tier
        ▼
 Trainer population (Advisors)
        │  cascade to the field
        ▼
   Wider field (Practitioner / Foundational)
```

## Roles

- **Sponsor** — leadership owner who ratified the Articles and owns the outcome signals.
- **Program lead** — owns the instantiated program and the cascade plan.
- **Trainers (Advisors)** — the population being enabled to enable others.
- **Field** — the sellers and technical staff the trainers will reach.

## How to run it

### A1. Select and validate the trainer population
- Identify candidates capable of reaching the **Advisor** tier in the relevant competency map.
- Validate them by demonstration (Article V), not nomination — Advisor means *can perform under
  complexity and can coach others*.

### A2. Enable the trainers to Advisor tier
- Deliver the instantiated program to the trainer population, covering both the competencies
  themselves and **how to teach and assess them**.
- Use `templates/session-plan-template.md` to structure each session.
- Ensure trainers are fluent in the AI-fluency competencies (S6–S8 / T6–T8), since they will model
  responsible AI use to the field.

### A3. Equip trainers to assess
- Hand trainers the assessment instruments built from
  `templates/assessment-builder-template.md`, and calibrate them so scoring is consistent across
  trainers.

### A4. Cascade to the field
- Trainers deliver adaptive paths (Article IV) to the field — only the competencies each person
  has not yet demonstrated at the required tier.
- Maintain the per-partner competency record so the cascade does not re-teach proven capabilities.

### A5. Certify by demonstration
- The field is certified through demonstrations administered by the trainers (or by AI at higher
  maturity levels), recorded against the competency maps.

### A6. Report against outcomes
- Roll up results against the ratified outcome signals (Article VIII), not attendance or
  completion. Feed findings into the **Amend** stage of the lifecycle.

## Where AI raises the ceiling

The cascade is where Path A intersects the maturity model. As the organization climbs:

- **L2:** AI assembles adaptive paths for the field, so trainers spend time coaching rather than
  sequencing content.
- **L3:** AI administers and scores demonstrations, removing the assessment bottleneck that
  normally limits how many people each trainer can certify.
- **L4–L5:** content stays current automatically and the system learns which trainer practices
  correlate with field outcomes.

The cascade multiplies reach; AI multiplies it again by removing the per-trainer ceiling on
delivery and assessment.

## Common failure modes

- **Nominating trainers instead of validating them.** An Advisor who cannot actually perform
  propagates gaps at scale. Validate by demonstration.
- **Cascading content instead of capability.** If trainers pass along material rather than build
  demonstrated competency, the program reverts to Article I's failure mode.
- **No outcome signal.** Without a ratified outcome measure, the cascade optimizes for throughput
  and the value is invisible to leadership.

## Graduating and adapting

Path A is the higher-leverage path, but it depends on trainer capacity. If that capacity is thin,
run **Path B** (`playbook/path-b-self-serve.md`) for parts of the field while you build a trainer
population, then converge on the cascade.
