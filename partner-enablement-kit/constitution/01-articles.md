# Constitution — The Articles

> **Constitutional layer.** The eight Articles are the durable core of the framework. They are
> technology-agnostic and intended to remain stable across product cycles and vendor changes.
> Change them only through the amendment process (`constitution/03-amendment-process.md`).

These Articles are the standard. Everything else in the kit — the maturity model, the
assessment, the competency maps, the playbook — is an instantiation or enforcement of them. An
adopting organization should be able to trace every enablement decision back to one of these
eight principles.

---

## Article I — Capability over Content

**Enablement is measured by what a partner can do, not by what they have consumed.**

The unit of enablement is a demonstrated capability, not a completed course, a viewed video, or
a passed quiz. Programs are designed backward from the outcomes a partner must be able to produce
in front of a customer. Content exists only in service of capability and is disposable; the
capability is what endures.

*Implication:* completion metrics are diagnostic at best and misleading at worst. Track
demonstrated competence.

---

## Article II — Technology Agnosticism

**Standards are expressed in terms of customer value and partner outcomes, decoupled from any
specific product, version, or vendor.**

A competency such as "can qualify a fit-for-purpose opportunity" or "can design an integration
that meets the customer's reliability requirement" is durable. The product through which the
partner does it is not. By writing the standard against the outcome, the framework survives
product releases, repositioning, and even changes of vendor.

*Implication:* product- and version-specific material lives in the instantiation layer, where it
is expected to churn. The constitution never names a product.

---

## Article III — Continuous Currency

**Enablement content has a half-life and must be continuously regenerated, not periodically
rebuilt.**

Content is treated as a perishable, regenerable asset rather than a durable one. The system is
designed so that material is refreshed continuously against the current state of the product and
the market — increasingly by AI — rather than rebuilt from scratch on an annual or per-release
cadence.

*Implication:* invest in the *pipeline* that regenerates content, not in any single artifact the
pipeline produces.

---

## Article IV — Adaptive Pathways

**Every partner receives a path adapted to their role, maturity, vertical, and prior
demonstrated competence.**

There is no single linear curriculum. The framework rejects one-size-fits-all by design: a
partner's path is assembled to fit who they are and what they have already proven. As personalization
at scale becomes the binding constraint, AI assumes responsibility for assembling and sequencing
each path.

*Implication:* the system must know what each partner has already demonstrated, so it never
re-teaches a proven capability or skips an unproven one.

---

## Article V — Demonstrated Competency

**Certification means demonstrated ability under realistic conditions, not recall.**

A partner is certified by performing — in a scenario, simulation, structured role-play, or
real-world artifact — not by selecting the correct multiple-choice answer. Assessment evaluates
judgment and execution against the outcomes defined in the competency maps. AI increasingly
administers and scores these demonstrations, making rigorous assessment affordable at scale.

*Implication:* assessment design is a first-class deliverable, not an afterthought bolted onto
content.

---

## Article VI — AI as Co-Enabler

**AI is embedded both in the delivery of enablement and in the work the partner is being enabled
to perform.**

The framework uses AI on two fronts at once. First, AI operates the enablement system —
authoring, personalizing, delivering, and assessing. Second, partners are explicitly enabled to
use AI in their own selling and solutioning, because that is how the work is now done. A partner
who cannot use AI in discovery, positioning, or solution design is not fully enabled, regardless
of product knowledge.

*Implication:* "AI fluency in the role" is a standing competency in both the sales and technical
maps, not a separate elective.

---

## Article VII — Federated Sovereignty

**Principles are defined centrally and enforced consistently, yet interpreted locally.**

The constitution is shared; its instantiation is sovereign to each adopting organization. An
organization expresses the principles in its own language, brand, vertical context, and product
set, and may add local statutes — provided they do not contradict the Articles. The accelerating
partner maintains the constitutional core; downstream organizations fork, customize, and operate
it; improvements flow back upstream as proposed amendments.

*Implication:* customization is a feature, not a deviation. The line that must not be crossed is
contradiction of an Article, not local adaptation of one.

---

## Article VIII — Measured Accountability

**Enforcement is tied to outcomes that matter, not to vanity metrics.**

The framework holds itself accountable to signals connected to real partner and customer results
— qualified pipeline, win rates on fit-for-purpose deals, successful implementations, customer
retention — rather than to course completions, attendance, or satisfaction scores alone. Vanity
metrics are permitted only as leading indicators, never as the definition of success.

*Implication:* every program must declare, in advance, the outcome signal by which it will be
judged.

---

## Reading the Articles together

The Articles are mutually reinforcing. Capability over Content (I) is meaningless without
Demonstrated Competency (V) to verify it and Measured Accountability (VIII) to connect it to
results. Technology Agnosticism (II) and Continuous Currency (III) are what make the framework
portable and durable. Adaptive Pathways (IV) and AI as Co-Enabler (VI) are where the asymmetric
scaling is realized. Federated Sovereignty (VII) is what lets a single standard travel across an
entire partner ecosystem without flattening the organizations within it.

static, manual enablement function to an AI-native one — and why that progression produces
