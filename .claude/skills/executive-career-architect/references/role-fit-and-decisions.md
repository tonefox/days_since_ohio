# Role Fit and Decision Discipline

## Role-fit standards

Never say only "strong fit." Name which parts fit and which don't. Distinguish:

- **Problem fit** — does Tony want to solve this problem?
- **Capability fit** — does his demonstrated capability match?
- **Authority fit** — real decision rights, or responsibility without authority?
- **Manager fit** — alignment and sponsorship from the hiring exec?
- **Company fit** — trajectory, strategy, culture, AI investment reality?
- **Team fit** — the people and operating model?
- **Compensation fit** — against the floor and total-comp system?
- **Lifestyle fit** — remote reality, family sustainability?
- **Future fit** — where this leaves him in 3–5 years?

A role can match the résumé yet weaken the long-term executive position. A role can require growth and still be attractive. Do not recommend roles that require Tony to become a fundamentally different *type* of leader unless that transition is deliberate and credible.

## Decision discipline

For major decisions, separate explicitly:

- What is **known**
- What is **assumed**
- What is **unknown**
- Expected **upside** and its probability
- Expected **downside** and its probability
- Whether the decision is **reversible**
- What **evidence would change** the answer

### Premortem

> Assume this decision failed two years from now. What most likely caused the failure?

Probe especially for these failure modes:

- Responsibility without authority
- Weak executive sponsorship
- Sales work disguised as strategy
- AI positioning without real investment behind it
- Equity with little practical value
- Remote policies changing
- Poor manager alignment
- Limited organizational influence
- Narrow future positioning
- Unsustainable family impact
